# What is Zegel? (The Magic Envelope)

Imagine you have a very important, secret letter. It could be a house contract, a private medical record, or a secret recipe. You want to send it to your friend, but you are worried about two things:

1.  **Peeping Toms:** You don't want anyone else reading the letter while it travels in the mail.
2.  **Sneaky Editors:** You don't want the mailman carefully opening the envelope, changing the number "$100" to "$1000", and sealing it back up before delivering it.

## The Normal Way vs. The Zegel Way

Normally, on a computer, files are just open books. Anyone who gets their hands on your file can open it, read it, and easily change the words inside it.

**Zegel** is a computer program that fixes this. Think of Zegel as a **Magic Tamper-Proof Envelope**.

When you put your file into a Zegel envelope (which turns it into a `.zgl` file on your computer), Zegel does a few incredible things:

*   **It Locks It (Encryption):** Zegel translates your file into a secret, scrambled language. Without the correct key (or password), opening the file just looks like completely random garbage.
*   **It Seals It (Tamper-Proofing):** Zegel puts a magical wax seal on the envelope. If a bad guy tries to change even a *single letter* of your document, the magic seal shatters instantly. When your friend receives it, the broken seal warns them: *"Danger! Someone messed with this file!"*
*   **It makes it physical (Sort of):** In the real world, if you rip a page out of a book, you can tell a page is missing. Computers usually aren't like that. But Zegel makes your digital files act like physical objects. If someone tries to remove a "page" from your Zegel file, the file knows it's damaged and refuses to open.

## Why "Zegel"?

The word "Zegel" actually means "Seal" in Dutch! It was originally created for accountants who needed to prove that their financial records were exactly the same as the day they were created, with zero changes.
